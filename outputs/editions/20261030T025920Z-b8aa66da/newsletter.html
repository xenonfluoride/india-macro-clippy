01

Finmin pegs Q2 expansion at 7.35%,flags capital pressure over US yield

The finance ministry estimates that India's economy grew 7.35% in the September quarter, supported by industrial output, GST collections, energy use, port traffic and auto sales. It expects domestic demand and investment to sustain momentum, while flagging high crude prices, food inflation, rupee pressure and elevated US bond yields as second-half risks.

Why it matters
Strong activity can coexist with tighter household budgets and more expensive capital when food, energy and external financing pressures rise. Watch whether the eventual GDP release and subsequent activity indicators confirm the ministry's estimate as these cost pressures evolve.

02

Fresh rules issued for dredging in forest areas to mitigate floods

The Centre has issued guidelines allowing scientifically planned river dredging in forest areas to mitigate floods and restore river ecosystems without treating the activity as a non-forest purpose. Qualifying works are exempt from forest clearance requirements, compensatory afforestation and net present value payments.

Why it matters
Removing these requirements can reduce the approval and payment burden for qualifying works, making the boundary of eligibility important for environmental oversight. Watch which dredging projects qualify and how their scientific plans and ecological outcomes are documented.

03

Govt extends Amnesty Scheme under ESIC for three month till December 31

The government has extended the amnesty scheme under ESIC until December 31. The ministry describes the New Amnesty Scheme 2025 as a one-time dispute-resolution initiative to reduce court-case backlogs and promote compliance under the erstwhile Employees' State Insurance Act; cases filed up to March 31, 2025 are eligible.

Why it matters
A longer settlement window can give eligible employers more time to resolve litigation and reduce uncertainty around compliance liabilities. Watch the number and value of eligible disputes settled before the extended deadline.

RSS · Economic Times Economy, 29 Oct · 08:38 UTC

04

Karnataka hikes Nandini milk prices by ₹8 per litre from November 1: State minister

Karnataka is increasing Nandini milk prices by ₹8 per litre from November 1, according to a state minister. The minister said the government considered demands from farmers and their representatives before finalising the increase.

Why it matters
A higher per-litre milk price raises household food spending, while the benefit to farmers depends on how the additional revenue is distributed. Watch the effective retail prices and any disclosed change in payments to milk producers when the increase takes effect.

05

Supreme Court rejects Mahua Moitra’s plea on AIF-FPI ownership disclosure

The Supreme Court rejected Mahua Moitra's plea on AIF-FPI ownership disclosure. The court said there is presently no legal or regulatory framework mandating public disclosure of their beneficial owners, and its order would not prevent the Centre or SEBI from framing a policy.

Why it matters
The decision leaves the design of a public-disclosure requirement with policymakers rather than creating one through this plea. Watch for a government or SEBI proposal specifying what beneficial-ownership information would become public and under which conditions.

RSS · Hindustan Times India, 30 Oct · 02:11 UTC

06

Accused must explain source of excess wealth, not investigators: Supreme Court

The Supreme Court ruled that public servants must explain wealth exceeding their known income once the prosecution establishes a disproportionate-assets case against them.

Why it matters
After the prosecution establishes the case, documented sources of a public servant's wealth become central to answering the allegation. Watch how subsequent cases apply that threshold and assess the explanations offered for excess wealth.

07

Bengaluru-based Inkle acquires Finally’s bookkeeping arm, expands team to 160

Bengaluru-built, San Francisco-headquartered Inkle has acquired Finally's bookkeeping division in an all-cash deal for an undisclosed amount. The acquisition takes its team to about 160 people across five countries and extends its business from startups to non-tech small businesses.

Why it matters
Buying an existing bookkeeping operation can broaden Inkle's customer base while adding integration demands across a larger international team. Watch whether Inkle retains the acquired clients and integrates the expanded team while serving its broader small-business market.
